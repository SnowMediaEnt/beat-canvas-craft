# Cornell Note-Taking System

Handout: [Cornell-Note-Taking-System.pdf](resources/Cornell-Note-Taking-System.pdf) (adapted from *How to Study in College*, Walter Pauk, 2001).

## Page layout
| Area | Size | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| **Notetaking column** (right) | 6" wide | Notes taken during the lecture |
| **Cue column** (left) | 2½" wide | Questions and key words, added after class |
| **Summary** (bottom) | 2" tall | A short summary of the page, written after class |

## The 5 R's
1. **Record:** during the lecture, write notes in the notetaking column in short "telegraphic" sentences.
2. **Questions:** as soon after class as you can, write questions in the cue column based on your notes. This clarifies meaning, shows relationships, builds continuity and strengthens memory. It also sets you up for exam studying.
3. **Recite:** cover the notetaking column. Looking only at the cue column, say the answers out loud in your own words.
4. **Reflect:** ask yourself: What's the significance of these facts? What principle are they based on? How can I apply them? How do they fit with what I already know? What's beyond them?
5. **Review:** spend **at least 10 minutes every week** reviewing all your previous notes.

## How we'll use it
Your class notes here can follow the same pattern. For each topic I'll add **cue questions** and a **summary**, so the study guide doubles as "recite" practice. That matches the weekly review in the 5th R.
